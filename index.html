a small ecosystem with big opinions

🌍 You don't control the creatures. You shape the ground.

🧺 Paint stone and water, harvest and fertilize – nutrients and your effort are finite.

❄️ Winter is coming. It always is.

📜 Rules – one sentence each, we promise

Gobblers (A)Mulchers (B)Nippers (C) nutrientwastestonewater
  1. Nutrient and waste spread to neighbouring cells every tick: slowly through soil, fast through water, never through stone.
  2. Waste on soil slowly rots into nutrient – fast in summer, not at all in winter.
  3. Gobblers (A) eat nutrient and leave waste.
  4. Mulchers (B) eat waste and leave nutrient.
  5. A hungry Nipper (C) eats an adjacent Gobbler, shares the meal with its Nipper neighbours and leaves waste.
  6. Nipper seeds only land next to Gobblers.
  7. Birth: an empty soil cell with exactly 3 neighbours of one species and food in the cell gets a new creature.
  8. A well-fed creature sometimes shoots a seed that lands as a small figure (clover, blinker or glider) on free, food-rich soil nearby.
  9. Survival: 2–3 neighbours of the same species and energy above zero.
  10. Every tick a creature eats its fill from its cell, pays upkeep in energy and leaves its product.
  11. Too much waste in the cell drains energy from Gobblers and Nippers.
  12. Winter raises upkeep for everyone.
  13. Drought: water behaves like soil for a while.
  14. Flood: water spreads out and washes away nearby nutrient and waste.
  15. Stone, water, harvesting and fertilizing all cost effort per cell from one slowly refilling budget; erasing to soil is free.
  16. Nobody lives on stone or in water – whoever gets painted over is gone.
  17. Harvest moves half of a cell's nutrient and waste into the reservoir.
  18. Fertilize pours the reservoir back as nutrient – there is no free nutrient.
  19. Health = average energy × average age (normalised); size scores nothing.
  20. If an active species dies out, the run is over.
